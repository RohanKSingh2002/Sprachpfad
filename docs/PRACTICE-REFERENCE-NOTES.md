## Recommended original practice taxonomy

1. **Grammar workshop:** a coherent group of 5-8 short items on one precise target. Cover conjugation, articles/cases, pronouns and prepositions. Give context that makes each expected answer unambiguous. Use a base verb or constrained word bank where needed.
2. **Text gaps:** one independently written message, email or short account with several blanks. Supply a small word bank, but explain whether words can be reused. Check each blank separately and show the complete text only after checking/reveal.
3. **Sentence workshop:** arrange chunks, join two clauses using a named connector, or transform a sentence according to one explicit instruction. Chunk ordering must accept authored valid alternative orders or clearly fix the opening phrase.
4. **Everyday reading:** an original notice, timetable, advertisement or message with 3-5 detail questions. Closed-choice distractors should be plausible but contradicted by or unsupported by the text. Include the evidence in feedback.
5. **Dialogue and functions:** choose or match a suitable reply, order a short exchange, or change a familiar request into a polite request. Keep situational cues visible, including who is speaking and the degree of formality.
6. **Vocabulary in use:** match a word with its meaning or function, form a collocation, or choose a suitable word in a sentence. Avoid ambiguous repeated English translations within a matching set; do not always reverse the matching column.
7. **Listening for information:** original recorded messages with detail selection or note completion. Let learners replay, and make the transcript an explicit support/review control. Do not reuse publisher audio. If new recordings are unavailable, be clear about browser-voice availability rather than presenting silent exercises.
8. **Guided writing:** an original everyday situation, 3 required communication points and an approximate length. Preserve the learner's response; show one possible model and a checklist. Do not grade open responses by exact string comparison or claim that clicking a checklist verifies correctness.

## Level progression

- **A1:** short single-clause language, present tense and common everyday functions; direct factual reading; names/numbers/time; a single controlled grammar target; brief practical messages. Avoid starting with a random advanced item from the entire level.
- **A2:** past events, planning, comparisons, explanations and common subordinate clauses; more connected text; notices and simple schedules; controlled transformations with context; messages requiring several linked points.
- **B1:** connected accounts, opinions and reasons; inference based on explicit evidence; cohesive use of connectors, infinitives and relative clauses; choosing register; practical responses and short reasoned writing. Keep B1 task complexity distinct from simply using rarer vocabulary.

## Interaction and feedback requirements

- Begin with a browsable set library organised by theme and format, showing the skill target and item count. Retain level selection.
- Each set has a real end, a visible completed/total indicator, and a review screen. Do not silently refill an endless queue.
- Preserve typed text and selection state after checking. Keep the prompt and feedback together. Show the learner's answer, a correct answer/model and a short reason.
- Closed tasks need deterministic answer validation, explicit accepted alternatives, and tolerance for harmless spaces/punctuation and existing ae/oe/ue/ss conventions. Do not hide genuinely different grammar behind overly broad normalisation.
- Separate first-attempt accuracy from practice after hints or answer reveals. Retry mistakes as a separate round without erasing the initial result.
- Log a completed response once. Repeated Check clicks, navigation or opening a model must not inflate calendar counts or accuracy.
- Support keyboard operation and visible focus. Never require dragging alone. All answer controls need labels and live feedback; shuffled matching items need stable identity.
- Changing topic or level should not accidentally apply an old answer to the new exercise. Reset active set state deliberately and explain in the interface if an unfinished round will be left.
- Writing is self-reviewed and should be visibly separate from scored questions. Retain the model as one possible response, not the only acceptable answer.

## Authoring guardrails

Write new scenarios, names, numbers, sentences, notices, dialogues, explanations, distractors and model answers. Use generic teaching methods from the references with the site's established design. Do not publish the scans, publisher recordings, source answer keys or distinctive page artwork. Review each original set for German accuracy, level fit, ambiguity and answer completeness before publication.
